## Top 5 Cybersecurity Stories You Should Know

1. **AT&T Customers Eligible for $7,500 in Data Breach Settlement** — *tl;dr:* AT&T customers whose personal data was compromised in two significant data breaches can file claims for compensation, potentially receiving up to $7,500. The breaches affected nearly all of AT&T's 109 million customers, with sensitive information such as call and text records and Social Security numbers being exposed. Customers are advised to check their eligibility and file claims promptly to secure their compensation. This settlement is part of a larger trend of accountability in the telecom industry regarding data protection practices.  
   ↪ <https://www.cbsnews.com/tag/data-breach/>
2. **Poland's Energy Sector Targeted by Coordinated Cyber Attacks** — *tl;dr:* Poland's renewable energy sector has been hit by a series of coordinated cyber attacks, significantly impacting its energy infrastructure. The attacks occurred during peak hours, raising concerns over national security and energy supply stability. Stakeholders in the energy sector are urged to enhance their cybersecurity measures and conduct thorough assessments of their systems to prevent future intrusions. This incident highlights the growing threat of cyber warfare against critical infrastructure, emphasizing the need for robust defense mechanisms and real-time monitoring to safeguard against such attacks.  
   ↪ <https://cyberpress.org/>
3. **Google Disrupts IPIDEA Proxy Network; Microsoft Patches Office Zero-Day CVE-2026-21509** — *tl;dr:* Google has dismantled the IPIDEA residential proxy network, which exploited user devices for cyberattacks, reducing its operational capacity significantly. Meanwhile, Microsoft issued urgent patches for a high-severity zero-day vulnerability in Office, tracked as CVE-2026-21509, that allowed attackers to bypass security features. Organizations are advised to update their systems promptly to mitigate risks associated with these vulnerabilities. Additionally, Ivanti addressed critical flaws in its Endpoint Manager Mobile software, while Poland attributed destructive cyberattacks on its power infrastructure to a Russian-linked threat group. Cybercriminals are also targeting exposed AI endpoints for resource hijacking.  
   ↪ <https://thehackernews.com/2026/02/weekly-recap-proxy-botnet-office-zero.html>
4. **Ransomware.live: Monitoring Victim Disclosures and Cyber Threats** — *tl;dr:* Ransomware.live is a platform dedicated to tracking and reporting victim disclosures from ransomware attacks. It continuously scrapes leak sites to provide real-time data on the latest victims, including sectors affected such as healthcare, technology, and finance. The site offers insights into negotiation tactics, ransomware groups, and statistics, helping organizations stay informed about ongoing cyber threats. Users are encouraged to leverage the platform's intelligence to enhance their cybersecurity measures and mitigate risks associated with ransomware attacks.  
   ↪ <https://www.ransomware.live/>
5. **AI-Driven Cyberattacks Set to Revolutionize Malware Landscape by 2026** — *tl;dr:* As artificial intelligence evolves, the cybersecurity landscape is bracing for a surge in sophisticated malware and cyberattacks by 2026. Experts predict the emergence of fully autonomous AI systems capable of executing entire attack lifecycles, leading to more targeted and personalized threats. Ransomware remains a primary concern, although its effectiveness may wane as AI-generated tools complicate extortion efforts. Organizations must enhance their defenses against both AI-driven malware and a potential resurgence of DDoS attacks, emphasizing the need for robust cloud-based protection and adaptive mitigation strategies.  
   ↪ <https://www.securityweek.com/cyber-insights-2026-malware-and-cyberattacks-in-the-age-of-ai/>
