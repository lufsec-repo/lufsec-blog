## Top 5 Cybersecurity Stories You Should Know

1. **SolarWinds Addresses Four Critical Vulnerabilities in Web Help Desk** — *tl;dr:* SolarWinds has released security updates for its Web Help Desk software, fixing four critical vulnerabilities that could lead to authentication bypass and remote code execution (RCE). The flaws include CVE-2025-40536, CVE-2025-40537, CVE-2025-40551, and CVE-2025-40552, with CVSS scores ranging from 7.5 to 9.8. Organizations using SolarWinds Web Help Desk are advised to apply these updates immediately to mitigate potential risks of unauthorized access and system compromise. Prompt action is essential to safeguard sensitive data and maintain operational integrity.  
   ↪ <https://thehackernews.com/>
2. **Ransomware Attacks Increasingly Target Smaller Organizations, Canadian Cybersecurity Agency Reports** — *tl;dr:* The Canadian Centre for Cybersecurity has reported a rise in ransomware attacks targeting smaller organizations with limited technology resources. Cybercriminals, primarily Russian-speaking groups from former Soviet states, are employing multi-extortion tactics, which include not only locking data but also threatening to attack victims' customers and launching denial-of-service attacks if ransoms are not paid. To mitigate risks, organizations are advised to adopt basic cybersecurity practices such as keeping software updated and implementing multi-factor authentication.  
   ↪ <https://thelogic.co/briefing/ransomware-hackers-targeting-smaller-victims-national-cybersecurity-agency-says/>
3. **AI-Driven Ransomware Threats on the Rise in Canada, Cybersecurity Centre Warns** — *tl;dr:* The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security has reported a significant increase in ransomware attacks, with criminals leveraging artificial intelligence to enhance their targeting and operational efficiency. Organizations across Canada, including businesses, hospitals, and universities, are increasingly at risk as cybercriminals adopt sophisticated tactics such as multi-extortion methods and automated negotiations. To combat these threats, the Cyber Centre emphasizes the importance of basic cybersecurity practices, including software updates, multi-factor authentication, and data backups, while encouraging organizations to utilize available tools for network monitoring.  
   ↪ <https://www.squamishchief.com/national-news/criminals-using-ai-to-commit-ransomware-attacks-cybersecurity-centre-warns-11801206>
4. **AI-Driven Ransomware Attacks Surge, Warns Canadian Cybersecurity Centre** — *tl;dr:* The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security reports a rise in ransomware attacks fueled by artificial intelligence, making it easier for criminals to target organizations, including businesses, hospitals, and universities. The report highlights the increasing sophistication of these attacks, with cybercriminals using AI to identify vulnerabilities, develop malware, and automate negotiations. Organizations are urged to adopt basic cybersecurity practices, such as regular software updates and multi-factor authentication, to protect against these threats. The report emphasizes that no organization is immune, and ongoing collaboration between sectors is essential to combat this growing challenge.  
   ↪ <https://vernonmatters.ca/2026/01/28/criminals-using-ai-to-commit-ransomware-attacks-cybersecurity-centre-warns/>
5. **AI-Enhanced Ransomware Threats Rise in Canada, Cybersecurity Centre Reports** — *tl;dr:* The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security warns that criminals are increasingly using artificial intelligence to enhance ransomware attacks, making it easier to identify vulnerabilities and automate negotiations. The report highlights a growing trend in multi-extortion tactics, where cybercriminals not only encrypt data but also threaten to leak sensitive information. Organizations of all sizes, including businesses and hospitals, are at risk, emphasizing the need for robust cybersecurity practices. The centre advises regular software updates, multi-factor authentication, and continuous network monitoring to mitigate these evolving threats.  
   ↪ <https://www.thecanadianpressnews.ca/politics/criminals-using-ai-to-commit-ransomware-attacks-cybersecurity-centre-warns/article_beb87bbe-1c0f-5f15-8831-43aace6cc6b3.html>
