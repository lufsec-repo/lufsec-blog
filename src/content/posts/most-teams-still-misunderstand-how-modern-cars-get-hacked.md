# Most Teams Still Misunderstand How Modern Cars Get Hacked

A lot of people still talk about car hacking like it starts and ends with CAN bus. That is too narrow — and it leads defenders to miss the real attack paths.

Modern vehicle risk often starts earlier in the trust chain: the mobile app, the backend API, the telematics layer, the account and ownership flow, the token model, or the gateway between remote features and the car itself.

That is why stories like LufSec’s recent breakdown on [how one weak vehicle API can unlock a car](/one-weak-vehicle-api-can-unlock-a-car/) matter. The lesson is not just that one component failed. The lesson is that modern automotive attacks move across trust boundaries that many teams still treat as clean.

If you build, test, defend, or just want to understand connected vehicles better, you need a broader mental model than “watch the CAN bus.”

## Why this matters now

As NHTSA keeps emphasizing in its guidance on [vehicle cybersecurity](https://www.nhtsa.gov/technology-innovation/vehicle-cybersecurity), software-driven cars create risk far beyond one in-vehicle protocol. If you only think about one bus, you miss the system.
