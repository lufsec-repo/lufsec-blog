Most companies have no idea how much of their network is exposed to the internet — and attackers map it before they do. In the latest Inside Cyber Minds, I sat down at DEF CON with **Jeff Foley**, founder and leader of **OWASP Amass**, to break down how attack surface discovery really works.

## It's not "management" — it's intelligence

Jeff's core argument: the industry calls it *attack surface management*, but it's really **attack surface intelligence**. Finding exposed assets is the easy part — the value is turning that data into accurate, actionable intelligence. "Whoever turns it into the most accurate actionable intelligence is going to win," he says, "and it's not being done that great yet."

## The question most tools can't answer: *who owns this?*

Commercial ASM tools tell you *what* they found and *where* it is — but not **what it's doing, who owns it, or how to reach that owner**. Jeff's DEF CON talk this year was on exactly that gap: **ownership attribution**, tying a discovered asset to the real legal entity responsible for it.

## Why OWASP Amass exists

Amass started around 2018 while Jeff was responsible for tracking National Grid's internet-facing OT devices — "too many for a human to continuously track," so they automated it. He later donated it to OWASP to keep it free: "This wasn't us trying to make money. This was awareness — empowering the community." Version 5.1 was rebuilt from the ground up on a new **open asset model**, now enterprise-grade and handling **millions of discoveries per hour**.

## On AI in recon

Jeff is measured: AI adds value when it gives you "**1 + 1 = 3**" — analyzing data, finding gaps, reconciling conflicting results — not when you just hand it your old problem and expect magic. His warning on "AI-powered" ASM vendors: it often just creates **noise**, and "CISOs don't want noise, they want precise results they can act on."

## The biggest myth

"People think it's comprehensive." No single vendor has the whole picture — so **assume there's always more to discover**, question everything, and do your own digging.

## His advice to get started

Learn how the internet actually works under the hood (DNS, protocols), then jump into the Amass project and Discord — it's turnkey and beginner-friendly. And his career tip: **"Learn red, and bring it to blue — that's where the funding is."**
